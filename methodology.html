The Pranaclimb Methodology

QUESTIONS
& ANSWERS

Pranaclimb is a field-based, non-invasive framework that helps climbers understand effort, pacing and recovery through breathing, perceived exertion and heart-rate recovery. It is designed for climbing's intermittent efforts, expressive breathing and repeated opportunities for recovery.

Pranaclimb is a field-based breathing and recovery system for climbers. It translates breathing, effort, and recovery into clear performance insight — on real routes, without lab equipment.

It's built for climbing's intermittent efforts — anaerobic bursts, aerobic endurance, and quick recovery — accounting for dynamic movement, expressive breathing, postural constraints, and micro-rests.

RBA measures → CoPro coaches
The Remote Breathing Assessment records and interprets your core markers. The Coaching Protocol transforms those findings into practical cues, pacing and recovery strategies.

The Three Core Variables

  • 🫁 Breathing Rate (BR) – real-time effort signal
  • 🎯 RPE (CR-10) – perceived exertion, interpreted alongside BR
  • ❤️ HRR₆₀ – operational recovery marker
Pranaclimb uses operational field anchors and modelled estimates informed by exercise-physiology literature. It does not replace laboratory measurement of CP, RCP, mechanical power or W′.

CP and RCP are used as Pranaclimb field anchors for interpreting the transition from sustainable toward severe, unsustainable effort. They are not laboratory measurements unless formally tested.

⛰️ Critical Power (CP)

Pranaclimb's operational boundary anchor for sustainable versus progressively depleting effort.
CP field anchor ≈ BR 45 BPM, RPE 8
Individual calibration and climbing context may shift it.

🔥 Respiratory Compensation Point (RCP)

Pranaclimb's redline anchor for severe, generally unsustainable effort.
RCP field anchor >55 BPM, RPE 9–10
This is a field interpretation unless RCP has been measured in a laboratory.

The relationship between CP and RCP in climbing remains an area for validation. Posture, terrain, isometric work and the diaphragm's dual respiratory and stabilising role may influence observed breathing.

The Grey Zone is the Pranaclimb transition band between the CP and RCP field anchors — the edge of sustainability. Composure, breathing rhythm, movement and pacing can all influence how the climber manages this band.

Within the Grey Zone, the model estimates progressive depletion. Partial reconstitution is assigned only when effort genuinely falls below the climber's personal CP field anchor.

Small pacing, movement or breathing changes here may meaningfully influence a redpoint attempt.

W′bal is the remaining finite work capacity above CP — often explained as a power battery. Pranaclimb translates this concept into a transparent, rule-based field estimate: depletion is modelled above CP and reconstitution below CP. It is not a direct measurement of mechanical power, W′ or energy use.

Use the W′bal estimate to explore pacing, crux cost and recovery decisions; it does not predict performance with certainty.

Climbing is not steady-state exercise. Breath holds, grunts, screams, sighs and reset inhales can interrupt or compress observable breathing cycles. Pranaclimb records these acts and calculates Effective Breathing Rate as an interpretation heuristic when Raw BR alone may under-represent the expressive load.

Formula: Effective BR = Raw BR + Σ Expression Adjustments

Interpretation boundary

Effective BR is a Pranaclimb operational interpretation variable, not measured ventilation, oxygen consumption or metabolic cost. Proposed scream-density multipliers remain in model development and are not part of the current public rule set.

Cap rules: Sport/Trad: +15 BPM cap per 60s · Bouldering: no cap · Raw BR ≥60 BPM: no correction needed

HRR₆₀ is the heart-rate drop from effort cessation to 60 seconds. Pranaclimb uses it as an operational recovery marker, interpreted alongside BR return, effort, test conditions and repeated measures.

✅ ≥18 BPM drop

Strong / excellent recovery classification within the Pranaclimb field framework.

⚠️ 12–17 BPM

Moderate recovery. Consider BR return, effort context and whether more recovery time is needed.

🔴 <12 BPM

Slow recovery flag. This is a coaching prompt, not a diagnosis; consider context and repeat the measure.

HRR₆₀ informs recovery interpretation; it does not directly measure W′bal recharge or prove autonomic state.

The Pranaclimb RBA is a field assessment that records breathing, perceived effort, expressive breathing and recovery using simple, portable markers.

Core RBA — Approximately 10-Minute Field Session

  • 3 mins calm nasal baseline and recording check
  • 4 mins continuous CP-focused climb
  • 1 min defined maximal-crux analysis window
  • 1 min passive recovery → record HRR₆₀ + BR return ≤30 BPM
  • About 1 min immediate ratings, notes and data check

The Pranaclimb Coaching Protocol (CoPro) transforms assessment into action — turning RBA data into personalised, performance-driven breathing strategies.

🧭 Zone-Based Coaching

Breathing cues for CP, Grey Zone, and RCP.

💨 Expressive Breathing

Observe breath holds, grunts, screams and exhales, then test more useful timing and release strategies.

🌬️ Recovery Strategy

Use recovery breathing and pacing cues, then track HRR₆₀ and BR return over repeated sessions.

🧘‍♀️ Composure Under Load

Test cues that support rhythm, attention and decision-making near the Grey Zone.
