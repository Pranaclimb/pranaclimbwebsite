    Inside the Book
    What Pranaclimb Brings Together
    01

    Breathing Makes Effort Visible

    Breathing rate and breathing behaviour reveal changing effort, stress and recovery in ways climbers can hear, observe and apply in the field.

    02

    Movement Changes Breathing

    Wall angle, posture, bracing, grip intensity and movement timing continually reshape ventilation and respiratory mechanics.

    03

    Breath and Perception Interact

    Breathing frequency tracks perceived exertion, while attention, emotion and expectation influence how hard a climb feels.

    04

    Intensity Has a Respiratory Signature

    CP, RCP and W′bal provide a practical framework for recognising sustainable effort, redline intensity and the recovery between hard sequences.

    05

    Recovery Can Be Coached

    HRR₆₀, breathing-rate recovery and clearly selected breathwork practices help climbers use rests more deliberately.

    06

    Breathing Is Trainable

    Assessment, observation and practice turn breathing from background physiology into a skill climbers can understand and refine.

    The Author

    Annie Anderson

    Annie Anderson is a climber, yoga teacher, and breathwork coach with over 30 years of experience in climbing and yoga, and more than a decade of independent respiratory research.

    She is the creator of the Pranaclimb Methodology — a breath-led performance system integrating breathing rate, critical power (CP), W′bal modelling, and nervous system regulation for climbers.

    Her book Pranaclimb: Love, Breathe, Climb combines science, breathwork, and elite insights, offering unique practices for climbers. Published research on ResearchGate and SportRxiv.
