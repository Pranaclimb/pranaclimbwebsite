About Annie

A Life Shaped by Movement and Breath

Annie's life has followed one continuous thread: climbing, movement, breath and the search for what helps us adapt.

Annie Anderson began traditional climbing in 1991 on the polished limestone of Bristol's Avon Gorge. Climbing took her from the crags and sea cliffs of the UK to Arapiles and the Grampians in Australia, Joshua Tree and Red Rocks in the United States, and the celebrated European climbing areas of the Verdon Gorge, Buoux, Fontainebleau, El Chorro and Finale Ligure.

While living in Dubai for five years, Annie managed the UAE's first indoor climbing wall, designed its courses and worked as its lead instructor. After qualifying as a climbing instructor at Plas-y-Brenin in 1998, she combined teaching with exploration in the mountains of Wadi Bih, where she helped establish new routes more than 500 metres long. Swimming, diving, mountain biking and desert journeys became part of the same adventurous life.

After returning to Cornwall in 2003, Annie created Explore Climbing, leading yoga-climbing courses and guiding on Cornwall's sea cliffs. Alongside this work, she was Project Director for The Equilibrium Centre. She also became a paraglider pilot, spending extended periods climbing and camping beneath Stanage.

In 2006, Annie moved to Turkey with her husband, Mark. Their daughter, Annalisa, was born in 2008 and spent her early childhood at the foot of crags in Turkey, North Cyprus and Thailand over the following six years. In 2012, Annie completed her 200-hour Yoga Teacher Training in Istanbul. This was a turning point: breathing became not simply part of yoga, but a subject of sustained practical exploration.

Since returning to West Cornwall in 2014, Annie has researched the relationship between breathing, effort, movement and recovery in climbing. Surrounded by granite, sea cliffs and the Atlantic Ocean, she combines independent research with climbing, yoga, breathwork, cold-water swimming and coaching.

In May 2022, Annie was diagnosed with Stage 4 renal cell carcinoma, with metastases affecting her pelvis, lungs, liver and brain. Following immunotherapy and the removal of her right kidney in January 2025, she completed treatment in October 2025 and is now cancer-free. Her family, climbing, breathwork, yoga and daily ocean swimming remain central to her strength, gratitude and joy.

She founded the Pranaclimb Methodology as a field-based system using breathing rate (BR), perceived exertion (RPE) and 60-second heart-rate recovery (HRR₆₀) to help climbers understand intensity, pacing and recovery on the rock. Her forthcoming book, Pranaclimb: Love, Breathe, Climb, brings together more than three decades of embodied experience and independent research.

“Breath is our superpower.” Rock-climbing performance depends on balancing anaerobic bursts, aerobic endurance and recovery across complex terrain. Controlled breathing can produce measurable acute physiological changes, while regular pranayama practice may support longer-term autonomic, respiratory and cardiorespiratory adaptations.
